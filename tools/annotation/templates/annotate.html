How to annotate

  • The left column is the window: every sentence in it (the dark ones) must end up either in a link or marked no counterpart. The faded sentences before and after it are context; you may use them when a translation unit crosses the edge of the window.
  • The right column is the other text, around the place where the window should be. Use earlier / later or the search box (names and numbers are good search terms) if the matching passage is not there.
  • Link: click the sentences on the left and on the right that translate each other (click again to unselect; shift-click selects a range), then press L. Link the smallest groups that translate each other: 1-1 whenever possible; 1-2, 2-1, 2-2 … when the translator split or joined sentences. The sentences of a link must be consecutive.
  • A sentence whose content is in the other text, even with details left out or added, is linked. A sentence with no counterpart (cut by the translator, added by the translator, a footnote, a page number) is no counterpart N. On the right, sentences between your links that you did not link count as no counterpart automatically.
  • If a passage was rewritten so freely that the sentences do not correspond, link the whole passage on both sides as one group and tick loose.
  • Headings link to headings. Links must keep the order of the texts (they cannot cross).
  • The sentence boundaries are fixed. If a boundary is clearly wrong, select that sentence and press F to flag it, with a short note; then annotate as well as the boundaries allow.
  • Click a link number to select the whole link; Del removes it (or removes a no counterpart mark).
  • If the window is pre-filled, the links come from the aligner: they may be wrong or missing. Correct them. Links marked ? have a low score; J jumps to the next one. Do not trust the others blindly: a pre-filled link is often right, but check the sentence boundaries and the edges of every 1-2 and 2-1 link.
